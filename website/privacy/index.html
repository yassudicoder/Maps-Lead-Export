Privacy policy · the extension

Nothing leaves your computer.

Last updated 6 August 2026 · Maps Lead Export by TheOpenBox

Maps Lead Export turns the businesses Google Maps has already drawn in the tab you are looking at into a CSV you download. It keeps everything on your computer, has no account and no server, and makes no network requests of its own. This page explains that in full.

in_short

The short version.

Every business the panel collects comes from what Google Maps has already rendered in the tab you are looking at, while you browse. The extension never runs in the background and never opens places on its own.

What it collects is written to your browser’s own storage and stays there until you export it to a file yourself. It has no account to sign into, no server to talk to, and it sends nothing anywhere. It does not track you, show ads, or sell or share your data — because there is nowhere for that data to go.

chrome.storage.local

What it keeps, and where.

Everything the extension keeps lives in your browser, on your own machine:

  • the current collection session — up to 2,000 businesses you have browsed past;
  • your saved filter presets — up to 24, holding filter values only, with no rows and no place data;
  • your export column choices;
  • the exported-places index — a record of which places you have already exported, so a business is never put in front of you twice.

The exported-places index stores place identifiers and dates only — no names, no addresses, nothing about the business itself. It is capped at 50,000 places, the oldest dropped first, and you can inspect it, download it as a CSV, or press Forget everything to clear it, all from the options page. None of this is ever transmitted.

egress

What ever leaves your computer.

Two things, and only when you ask for them:

  • the CSV file you export, which Chrome saves to your own Downloads folder;
  • the diagnostics you copy, which go to your own clipboard.

Nothing else leaves the machine. There is no code in the extension that sends data to any server — it makes no network requests of its own.

permissions

Every permission, and why.

Each Chrome permission the extension requests, and what it is for.
activeTab Lets the panel read the Google Maps tab you are on — but only that tab, and only after you open the panel from the toolbar. It cannot see your other tabs.
scripting Runs the reader that takes the results already drawn on that Maps tab.
storage Keeps your session, presets, column choices and exported-places index on your own computer.
downloads Saves the CSV you export to your Downloads folder.
sidePanel Shows the panel beside Google Maps.

The extension asks for no host permissions, so it cannot run on websites at large, and it never visits third-party sites.

never

What it never does.

  • Run in the background, or collect anything while you are not looking at Google Maps.
  • Open places or move the page on its own — every action follows a fresh click of yours.
  • Read anything outside the Maps tab you are on, or visit third-party websites.
  • Collect email addresses or contact details the page does not already show.
  • Interact with a verification check — if Google shows one, it steps aside and asks you to solve it.
  • Show ads, require an account, or take payment.
  • Sell, share, or transmit what you collect. It has nowhere to send it.
analytics

Usage analytics.

Today the extension collects no usage analytics and sends nothing anywhere. If an optional analytics signal is ever added, it will be opt-in and off by default, and could only ever report anonymous feature counts — how often a feature was used — never the businesses you collect, your searches, or anything that could identify you or them.

your_controls

What you control.

Your collected leads, presets and the exported-places index are yours to clear at any time: Clear session empties the current session, and Forget everything on the options page erases the exported-places index. Removing the extension removes all of it. Because none of it ever left your computer, there is nothing held anywhere else to delete.

changes

Changes to this policy.

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Anything that materially changes what the extension does with your data ships as a versioned update you choose to install — never as a silent change on a machine you have not touched.
